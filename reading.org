#+title: Reading Cairn

~[open]~

Take your time with this.

Cairn is small. It is a record that carries direction across sessions
that forget. Everything in it is one shape, appearing at three sizes.
Once you see the shape, the rest follows.

What follows is the whole of Cairn, stated plainly. The axioms, the
laws, and the provisions are quoted in full. Each quote appears where
it is needed. By the end, every one has been read, and the shape is
visible.

* The foundation

Cairn rests on four natures, nine laws, and thirty-one necessities.
The natures are what the system is made of. The laws are what follows.
The necessities are what situations demand, and where their forms
come from.

Here they are together. Read them once. Everything after this is the
same three things seen from different sides.

** The natures

> *A1. The session is episodic and forgets.*
> A session begins, acts, ends. It does not persist.

> *A2. The custodian is continuous, transferable, and may be one or
> many.*
> Its continuity is established by the record. The core acknowledges
> the plurality of custodians; it does not govern their relations.

> *A3. There is a record.*
> It is persistent, shared, and legible to a cold reader.

> *A4. Whatever is not in the record does not exist.*
> Nothing depends on session memory.

> *B1. Two kinds of authoring.*

> *B2. Every entry carries a token.*
> The token carries the custodian's relation to the direction the

> *B3. The custodian's relation is distinguishable.*

> *B4. Four acts.*
> The custodian may choose, withdraw, and revise. The session may

> *B5. Authority boundary.*

Now the same three things, seen from the side where the reader stands.

* The natures

Start with A1.

> *A1. The session is episodic and forgets.*
> A session begins, acts, ends. It does not persist.

A session is not a continuous thing. It comes into being, it does
work, and it is gone. When it is gone, everything it held in mind is
gone with it. It does not remember the last session, or the one
before.

Read from the work's side, A1 says: a session cannot carry the work.
Read from the session's side, A1 says: I do not remember, and I will
not. Both are the same fact.

Something must carry the work. That is A2.

> *A2. The custodian is continuous, transferable, and may be one or
> many.*
> Its continuity is established by the record. The core acknowledges
> the plurality of custodians; it does not govern their relations.

A custodian is not episodic. A custodian is the role that carries the
thread. When the custodian reads the record at the start of a session,
the thread is unbroken — not in the custodian's memory, but in the
record. The custodian knows the last session because the record knows
it. When the custodian writes, they have already judged what they
write. When they read, they re-encounter something they may have
judged before.

The custodian is transferable because continuity is established by
the record, not by the person. A new custodian who inherits the
record inherits the full state and can judge from it. The custodian
may be one or many. The core acknowledges the plurality. It does not
govern how custodians arrange themselves among themselves.

Where does the custodian's continuity live? In the record.

> *A3. There is a record.*
> It is persistent, shared, and legible to a cold reader.

The session cannot hold the direction. The custodian's memory is
finite. But the work must survive. So there is a record.

The record is persistent: it survives the session that wrote it and
the custodian who keeps it. It is shared: more than one participant
can read it and write to it. It is legible to a cold reader: a reader
with no memory beyond the record can read it and understand what it
says.

And the record is the whole of what exists.

> *A4. Whatever is not in the record does not exist.*
> Nothing depends on session memory.

If a fact is not written, there is no one who could have kept it. The
session cannot. The custodian's memory is finite and personal. Only
the record persists. Whatever must survive must be written.

A4 is the floor. Everything else is built on it. Every mechanism in
Cairn must explain itself, because there is no one present who can
explain it. The record must be legible on its own.

The four natures are not premises. They are the situation stated four
times — from the session, the custodian, the record, and the rule
that binds them.

* Direction

The work moves. It has a path — proposals become decisions, open
questions become settled ones, some ways are taken and others are set
aside.

*Direction is that path.* It is the course the work is taking. It is
what the custodian chooses as they walk through the record.

Direction has two scales. At one entry, the direction is the
custodian's relation to that entry. Across the record, the direction
is the pattern of those choices — the whole path the custodian has
walked. The two scales are the same fact seen at two sizes.

Direction is the ground because of A4.

> *A4. Whatever is not in the record does not exist.*

The session forgets. The work must survive. If the direction lives
only in memory, it dies with the memory. If it lives only in a
session's head, it does not survive the session. So the direction
must be visible in the record. That is what the token does.

* The token — the pivot at its smallest

The record carries direction through tokens. A token is a small
marker at the start of an entry. It says one thing: the custodian's
relation to the direction the entry proposes.

> *B2. Every entry carries a token.*
> The token carries the custodian's relation to the direction the
> entry proposes.

Every entry — no matter what it says, no matter who wrote it, no
matter what kind of work it is — carries a token. The token sits at
the start of the entry.

Without the token, a reader cannot tell whether the custodian has
engaged the entry, and how. The reader cannot tell whether the entry
is a proposal waiting for judgment, or a decision already made, or a
direction that was released. The token is what makes the direction
visible.

** The token is a pivot

A pivot is a point where two things meet.

Above the token — what makes it meaningful. The laws say what the
token means, what may happen to it, and what may not. Without the
laws, the token is an empty mark.

Below the token — what it makes possible. The situations, the
necessities, the formations, and the templates unfold from the
token. The token bounds them; they give the token its reach.

The token is not the whole of Cairn. It is the point where the whole
of Cairn touches, at its smallest size.

** Who may set a token

> *B1. Two kinds of authoring.*
> The session authors proposals. The custodian authors decisions.

B1 says who may set a token and who may not. The session's writing
enters unengaged — a new entry written by a session carries ~[open]~.
The custodian's writing enters engaged. A reader can tell them apart
because the token names the kind.

** How many tokens

> *B3. The custodian's relation is distinguishable.*
> The set of relations is finite. Each relation carries a distinction
> no other relation carries.

B3 says the token must be unambiguous. Five values, five
distinctions, no overlap. The meta file chooses the finite set. In
this instance, there are five tokens.

- ~[open]~ — the direction is untouched. No custodian has engaged it.
- ~[held]~ — the custodian holds this direction. It is in their
  hands, being carried, being considered.
- ~[approved]~ — the custodian approves this direction. They have
  committed.
- ~[blocked]~ — the custodian blocks this direction. They have
  refused.
- ~[reopened]~ — the custodian has released this direction. It was
  engaged once. That relation is no longer in force. The direction is
  available again, with the history that it was once engaged.

** How the token changes

> *B4. Four acts.*
> The custodian may choose, withdraw, and revise. The session may
> revise directions the custodian has not engaged, or has withdrawn.

B4 says how a token changes. The custodian chooses a relation.
Withdraws a relation. Revises content and sets a relation. The
session revises content on a direction the custodian has not engaged
or has withdrawn, without changing the relation.

** Where the token's authority lies

> *B5. Authority boundary.*
> The session may cause only the unengaged direction. An engaged
> direction lies beyond the session by nature. The session may revise
> directions the custodian has not engaged, or has withdrawn. The
> custodian may set any relation.

The session can only cause ~[open]~. The custodian can cause any
relation. The boundary is not a rule the custodian enforces. It is
what the token's nature is.

The session's permission is not a separate rule. It is a consequence
of the custodian's relation. The word does the work. A reader who
sees ~[held]~ knows the direction is in someone else's hands, and does
not need a rule telling them to stay out.

** The one-way door

> *B6. The one-way door.*
> The unengaged direction is the only direction with no incoming edge
> from any other. Once a direction is engaged, it never returns to
> unengaged.

~[open]~ means never touched. ~[reopened]~ means touched, then
released. These are different facts. A reader who sees ~[open]~ knows
the custodian has never engaged the entry. A reader who sees
~[reopened]~ knows the custodian engaged once and released.

Once a direction is engaged, it never returns to ~[open]~. The one-way
door holds.

** Closure

> *B7. Closure.*
> An engaged direction is closed to sessions by nature. The closure
> forbids a class of participant from revising the direction or from
> creating a new entry on the same content. The custodian may lift
> the closure, and by lifting it, opens the direction again.

An engaged direction is closed. This is not a tool the custodian
picks up. It is what engagement is. When the custodian sets a
relation, the direction is closed to sessions by that fact. The
custodian may lift it, and by lifting, opens the direction again.

** Two rules of the token

Two rules keep the token the only marker of direction in the record.

*Rule one: every entry is wrapped in a token.*

Whatever the situation, whatever the shape of the content, whatever
the workflow — the entry is wrapped in a token. The token comes
first. The content comes after.

A session writes content. When the session is done, the content is
wrapped in ~[open]~. The token is not part of the content. It is the
wrapper. The custodian reads the entry. If they take it up, they
change the token to ~[held]~. The content does not change. Only the
token changes.

An entry without a token does not exist. No token, no entry.

*Rule two: the token words are reserved.*

The five token words belong to the tokens. They are reserved. No
field inside an entry uses them. No field inside an entry is called
*state*.

The token is the only marker of direction in the record. If content
fields also carried state-like words, the reader could not tell which
was the token. So the token words stay reserved. They mark the token
and nothing else.

When content needs to say what kind of thing it is, or what decision
was made, or what phase the work is in, it uses its own vocabulary.
*Kind: proposal.* *Decision: accepted.* *Phase: review.* Never a
token word. Never the word *state*.

The token at the top. The metadata in the middle. The content at the

* What unfolds from the token

The token is the pivot at its smallest. Everything else unfolds from
it.

A situation is a scene of work. A session writes an entry and wraps
it in ~[open]~ — that is a proposal-situation. A custodian reads the
entry and sets a token — that is a judgment-situation. One session
ends and another begins — that is a handoff-situation.

Every situation is bounded by tokens. The token marks the edge of the
scene. It says: here the situation began, here it ends, this is what
may pass through.

A situation demands conditions to proceed. A proposal demands that
the session know its role, that the token mean what it says, that the
custodian has not touched the entry. Those conditions are the
necessities.

And when a kind of situation recurs — many proposals, many judgments,
many handoffs — it is worth naming the kind. The name is a formation.
A formation carries a name, a structure, and the bundle of
necessities that kind of situation demands.

** The dual relation

A formation is the name of a kind of situation *and* the bundle of
necessities that kind demands. The two are not separate lists. They
are one fact: *this kind of situation demands these conditions*.

To know the formation is to know the necessities. To know the
necessities is to know the formation. To have both is to have the
direction answered.

** The chain of interpretation

The token is a direction. The direction alone is opaque. To read it,
the reader follows a chain. Each step interprets the previous one.

**Direction.** The token. The custodian's relation to what is here.

**Situation.** What scene the direction sits in.

**Necessities.** What that scene demands.

**Formation.** The name for the kind of scene, carrying both the
scene and its conditions.

**Templates.** The shapes that give the direction a body.

Read forward, the chain is the reading of a token. Read backward, it
is how a session writes.

* The basic form — the pivot at its middle size

The chain is walkable because every step is held in the same form.
The form is the *token-marked entry*: a token, metadata, content.

The token-marked entry is the pivot at its middle size. Above it —
the four stances that approach it. Below it — the chain that unfolds
from it.

The form says only what must be true for any direction to be legible.
The token says where it stands. The metadata says who wrote it and
when. The content says what. Nothing else is required.

The form is universal. Every mode uses the same form.

- An *Anchored* provision lives in an entry. The form is basic,
  provided by the guide. The meta shapes it.
- A *Named* provision lives in an entry. The form is chosen by the
  instance.
- An *Allowed* provision lives in an entry. The form is the
  custodian's practice.
- A *Carried* provision lives in an entry. The form comes from
  another system.

The mode changes who provides the form. The entry carries the form in
every case. That is what makes the four modes comparable — they are
all expressed in the same vessel.

That is also what makes the chain of interpretation walkable across
natures. A reader does not have to switch languages between
direction and situation, or between necessities and templates. Every
step is read from the same kind of thing.

A session walks the chain to write. It picks templates, finds the
formation, sees the necessities, names the situation, wraps the
entry in ~[open]~.

A custodian walks the chain to judge. They see the token, read the
content, know the situation, see what it demands, and choose a
relation.

A cold reader walks the chain to understand. They read the tokens,
follow the content, and know where the work stands.

* The provisions, in full

A situation demands conditions to proceed. The conditions are the
necessities. Each is made true by a provision — a practical choice.
The provision takes one of four modes, distinguished by where the
form comes from.

Cairn's own nature does four things at once. It *anchors* the needs
its own natures prove, providing basic forms the meta will shape. It
*names* the needs its own natures prove but whose forms it cannot
choose, asking the instance to answer. It *permits* the needs that
situations demand without its own natures proving them, leaving the
custodian to provide or not. It *acknowledges* other natures —
natures it does not own — by providing the vessel in which their
directions are carried, recorded, and made legible.

* The threshold — the pivot at its largest

The token is the pivot at its smallest. The basic form is the pivot
at its middle. At the largest scale, the pivot is the threshold
between the guide and the meta.

The guide contains the axioms, the laws, and the modes of provision.
It states the natures. It states what follows. It states where each
necessity finds its form. And it provides one universal thing: the
vessel.

The meta is the practical interpretation. It chooses the tokens. It
chooses the files. It shapes the Anchored forms into walkable paths.
It provides the forms for the Named necessities. It practices the
Allowed ones. It records the traces of the Carried ones.

The threshold is where the guide stops and the meta begins. Above
it — the guide, the natures, the laws. Below it — the meta, the
practical forms, the working record.

At the threshold, the four stances meet. The token-marked entry is
the vessel they all use. The token is the pivot they all pass
through.

Most systems choose one posture. Total specification. Total silence.
Hidden choices. Cairn takes four stances at once and names them. For
some needs, Cairn provides a basic form. For others, it proves the
need and asks for the form. For others, it permits the need. For
others, it acknowledges the need and provides only the vessel.

The distinctiveness is not that Cairn provides a little and asks for
a lot. The distinctiveness is that Cairn *knows which is which*,
*says so*, and *makes the division legible*. A reader can see, for
each necessity, which nature proved it, who provides the form, and
where the form lives. Nothing is hidden. Nothing is over-claimed.
