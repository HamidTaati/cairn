#+title: Cairn

~[open]~

This file holds the foundation. Four axioms, nine laws, thirty-one
necessities in four modes of provision. These are the natures Cairn
is built from, what follows from them, and where each necessity finds
its form.

Everything else is interpretation. This is what is interpreted.

* Axioms

** A1. The session is episodic and forgets.
A session begins, acts, ends. It does not persist.

** A2. The custodian is continuous, transferable, and may be one or many.
Its continuity is established by the record. The core acknowledges
the plurality of custodians; it does not govern their relations.

** A3. There is a record.
It is persistent, shared, and legible to a cold reader.

** A4. Whatever is not in the record does not exist.
Nothing depends on session memory.

* Laws

** B1. Two kinds of authoring.
The session authors proposals. The custodian authors decisions.

** B2. Every entry carries a token.
The token carries the custodian's relation to the direction the entry
proposes.

** B3. The custodian's relation is distinguishable.
The set of relations is finite. Each relation carries a distinction
no other relation carries.

** B4. Four acts.
The custodian may choose, withdraw, and revise. The session may revise
directions the custodian has not engaged, or has withdrawn.

** B5. Authority boundary.
The session may cause only the unengaged direction. An engaged
direction lies beyond the session by nature. The session may revise
directions the custodian has not engaged, or has withdrawn. The
custodian may set any relation.

** B6. The one-way door.
The unengaged direction is the only direction with no incoming edge
from any other. Once a direction is engaged, it never returns to
unengaged.

** B7. Closure.
An engaged direction is closed to sessions by nature. The closure
forbids a class of participant from revising the direction or from
creating a new entry on the same content. The custodian may lift the
closure, and by lifting it, opens the direction again.

** B8. Preservation is append-only.
A session's last act is to preserve. No history is rewritten. What
came before is preserved.

** B9. Sovereignty.
The custodian governs. The guide governs in full. Meta files govern
to the extent engaged. Unconfirmed content binds no one.

* Provisions

A situation demands conditions to proceed. The conditions are the
necessities. Each is made true by a provision. The provision takes
one of four modes, distinguished by where the form comes from.

Cairn's own nature does four things at once. It *anchors* the needs
its own natures prove, providing basic forms the meta will shape. It
*names* the needs its own natures prove but whose forms it cannot
choose, asking the instance to answer. It *permits* the needs that
situations demand without its own natures proving them, leaving the
custodian to provide or not. It *acknowledges* other natures —
natures it does not own — by providing the vessel in which their
directions are carried, recorded, and made legible.

** C1. Anchored

The guide's nature proves the need, and the guide provides a form —
a very basic one. The meta shapes it into a walkable path and may
build more complex formations on top, using this basic form.

*** C1.1 Continuity
The session forgets; nothing survives in the session. The guide
proves the need and provides a basic form: the direction must be
written. The meta shapes it — the record, the preservation, the
files.

*** C1.2 Authority
A direction must have a chooser. The guide proves the need and
provides a basic form: there is one who decides. The meta shapes it —
the custodian, the role, the transfer.

*** C1.3 Lifecycle
The custodian's relation must be distinguishable and finite. The
guide proves the need and provides a basic form: the five tokens. The
meta names them and shapes them into the lifecycle.

*** C1.4 Meaning
The tokens must be interpretable. The guide proves the need and
provides a basic form: each token carries one meaning. The meta
states the meanings and reserves the words.

*** C1.5 One-way door
Engagement must be irreversible. The guide proves the need and
provides a basic form: an engaged direction never returns to
unengaged. The meta shapes it into the rule that reopened is distinct
from open.

*** C1.6 Closure
A relation may close a direction to a class. The guide proves the
need and provides a basic form: an engaged direction is closed by
nature. The meta shapes it into the closure and the custodian's lift.

*** C1.7 Preservation
What came before must remain legible. The guide proves the need and
provides a basic form: no history is rewritten. The meta shapes it —
the record, the append-only rule, the commit.

*** C1.8 Sovereignty
Governance must have a single source. The source is the custodian
role, not the person who holds it. The guide proves the need and
provides a basic form: the custodian governs. The meta shapes it —
who the custodian is, how the role is held.

** C2. Named

The guide's nature proves the need, and the guide asks for the form.
The meta provides it.

*** C2.1 Identity
An entry must remain referable across change. The guide names the
need — custodianship transfers, history is preserved. The meta
provides the form: slugs, dates, property drawers.

*** C2.2 Provenance
The record must show who caused what. The guide names the need — two
kinds of authoring, the boundary is exact. The meta provides the
form: the `By` field, the custodian's name, the transfer record.

*** C2.3 Relations
Entries may connect. The guide names the need — a record is not a
pile. The meta provides the form: links, dependencies, supersessions.

*** C2.4 Time
The record may carry temporal facts. The guide names the need — the
reader must know when. The meta provides the form: dates, deadlines,
sequences.

*** C2.5 Scope
The record may have boundaries. The guide names the need — the reader
must know what an entry applies to. The meta provides the form:
files, placement, the reading order.

*** C2.6 Memory
What persists and what does not must be distinguishable. The guide
names the need — the session does not persist, the record persists,
and the custodian's continuity is established by the record. The meta
provides the form: the record, the resume, the handoff.

*** C2.7 Context
The surroundings of an entry may matter. The guide names the need —
an entry read in isolation is partial. The meta provides the form:
the file, the neighbours, the links.

*** C2.8 Evolution
The system may change over time. The guide names the need — the guide
is not frozen, the meta can grow. The meta provides the form:
amendment, adoption, retirement.

** C3. Allowed

The guide's nature permits the need that different situations demand.
The guide provides no form. The custodian provides it, if the
situation calls.

*** C3.1 Argument
Proposals may be structured. The guide permits this. The custodian
provides the form, if proposals need structure to be judged.

*** C3.2 Attention
The custodian must direct focus. The guide permits this. The custodian
provides the form, if the workflow grows past what can be held in
mind.

*** C3.3 Retrieval
The record must be findable. The guide permits this. The custodian
provides the form, if the record grows past what can be read in a
sitting.

*** C3.4 Collaboration
Multiple participants may propose. The guide permits this. The
custodian provides the form, if more than one session works on the
record.

*** C3.5 Error
Failures must be recognizable. The guide permits this. The custodian
provides the form, if mistakes are possible and worth naming.

*** C3.6 Recovery
Lost work must be restorable. The guide permits this. The custodian
provides the form, if history is available and restoration is a real
need.

*** C3.7 Onboarding
New participants must begin. The guide permits this. The custodian
provides the form, if new readers arrive.

*** C3.8 Feedback
Proposals and decisions may exchange. The guide permits this. The
custodian provides the form, if the dyad needs to respond to itself.

** C4. Carried

Another nature proves the need, with different situations. The guide
provides the vessel — the basic form of five tokens and the recording
of them. That basic form makes the walkable path reachable for those
situations, and recording preserves them.

*** C4.1 Formality
Formal artifacts may be attached as content. The need is proven by
the nature of formal methods. The guide provides the vessel: an entry
carries the artifact and the direction.

*** C4.2 Automation
Views and reports may be generated. The need is proven by the nature
of generation. The guide provides the vessel: an entry records what
was generated.

*** C4.3 Inter-custodian relations
Custodians may arrange their affairs among themselves. The need is
proven by the plurality of custodians — a nature Cairn acknowledges
but does not own. The guide provides the vessel: an entry records the
arrangement. The custodians provide the form. The core does not
govern how the single source is achieved when the role is held by
many. That is the custodians' own work.

*** C4.4 Distributed trust
Custodians may arrange trust among themselves. The need is proven by
the nature of distributed trust. The guide provides the vessel: an
entry records the verification.

*** C4.5 Real-time coordination
Simultaneity may be recorded after the fact. The need is proven by
the nature of concurrency. The guide provides the vessel: an entry
records what happened.

*** C4.6 Complexity
Complexity may be measured and the measure recorded. The need is
proven by the nature of complexity. The guide provides the vessel: an
entry records the measure.

*** C4.7 Emergence
Novel properties may be noted, not detected. The need is proven by
the nature of emergence. The guide provides the vessel: an entry
records the noting.

* The whole

Four axioms. Nine laws. Thirty-one necessities in four modes of
provision.

The axioms state the natures. The laws state what follows. The modes
state where each necessity finds its form.

In every mode, the vessel is the same. A token, metadata, content.
The token carries the direction. The mode says where the form comes
from. The vessel carries the form wherever it comes from.

The session forgets. The custodian is continuous. The record is the
medium. The record rule is the floor. From these four, everything
else follows.
